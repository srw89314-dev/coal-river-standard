# Lead-response playbook

## Response standard

- Reply during the same business day when practical.
- Personalize every response using the business name and the goal they submitted.
- Do not promise rankings, traffic, leads, revenue, or a launch date before reviewing the project.
- Move qualified leads toward one short conversation and a written proposal.

## First response

Subject: Coal River Standard — [Business name]

Hi [First name],

Thanks for telling me about [Business name]. I saw that you want the website to help with [briefly restate their goal]. I’m going to review the current online presence before suggesting a direction.

The next useful step is a few details about the business, the customers you most want to reach, and what visitors should be able to do. We can handle this by email, or arrange a short call if you prefer.

There is no obligation. If the project looks like a fit, I’ll send a clear scope, price, and timeline in writing before asking you to commit.

Sean  
Coal River Standard

## If the lead is not a fit

Hi [First name],

Thank you for reaching out. Based on what you need, I don’t think Coal River Standard is the right fit. I’d rather be straightforward than push you into the wrong project.

[Optional: one useful next step or referral, only if genuine.]

I appreciate you considering Coal River Standard.

## Follow-up after three business days

Hi [First name],

I wanted to follow up on the website conversation for [Business name]. If improving the site is still a priority, I’d be glad to continue by email or arrange a short call. If the timing is not right, no problem at all.

## Qualification notes

Before proposing work, confirm:

- The business and primary contact are real and reachable
- The requested site fits a package from [the approved menu](offer.md), or needs custom discovery
- The owner can provide lawful content and photos
- One person can approve the work
- The price and payment schedule are realistic for the prospect
- Any ecommerce, booking, legal, accessibility, or regulated-industry needs are identified early

## From inquiry to proposal

1. Check the business inbox and both Netlify Forms queues. Use the submission ID to avoid creating a second lead when a notification and its form record refer to the same inquiry. Review possible false positives before marking anything as verified.
2. Keep a private record of the business, contact, category, original goal, source, received date, current stage and next action. When the business workbook is in approved storage, use the matching client/contact/project references and put the follow-up in Actions.
3. Reply in the existing email thread after checking that Reply-To is the submitted address. Ask only the questions needed to decide fit. Email discovery is fine for simple builds. Use a short call when the scope, authority or integration needs discussion.
4. Route informational sites to the published page limits. For booking or shopping, confirm the existing platform, catalog or workflow first. Route accounts, migrations and unusual integrations to discovery before quoting. Route existing client issues to their existing project record instead of opening a sales lead.
5. When qualified, prepare a versioned [proposal](../client-packet/03_PROPOSAL.md) and [statement of work](../client-packet/04_STATEMENT_OF_WORK.md). Record who approves it and which content, access, tax treatment, vendor fees or support terms still need confirmation. Send a final quote only after pricing and scope are clear.
6. Set a follow-up for three business days after the first response. When a lead replies, update the next action. After the follow-up, leave the record waiting for the prospect or close it if they decline. A lead becomes an active paid project only after the reviewed agreement, agreed starting payment and required client inputs are in place.

Private preview inquiries are for controlled testing while sales intake awaits launch.
